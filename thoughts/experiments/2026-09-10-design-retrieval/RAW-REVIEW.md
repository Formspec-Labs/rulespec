# Manual source/result review

Reviewer: retrieval pilot agent, after scoring; unblinded because indexed source and summary shapes are visibly distinguishable. Labels are revisable. Reviewed all 16 queries against frozen source text and the top three returned rows in each arm. Exact hit texts, evidence spans and complete ranks are in `results.json`; complete indexed text is in `indexes.json`.

## Useful signals

- **L1, nonconsecutive employment:** Source ranks the actual (b) provision first; statement-only cannot retrieve it at any rank because no summary evidence includes that provision. Combined preserves it at rank 1 and adds the selected qualification from (b)(1). This is a real source provision not represented in this saved extraction, not the constructed no-statements control. It supports retaining source retrieval independently of extraction.
- **A1, beer as cargo:** The source query retrieves the possession prohibition but not the separate `Manifested and transported as part of a shipment` list item: none of the query's words occurs in that short item. The faithful grouped statement connects the shipment exception to beer and retrieves the needed support. Combined also succeeds, but its top three hits are the prohibition, shipment and passenger fragments, all expanded to essentially the same grouped meaning. One useful result occupies three result slots.
- **A3, written appeal deadline:** Source ranks the actual petition provision third, behind the generic reporting lead-in and no-violation duty. Statements and combined rank it first. This is a bounded useful lexical ranking improvement for a paraphrase query.
- **L4, eligibility conjunction:** Source retrieves the hours condition but not the full selected conjunction. The grouped statement retains the 12 months, 1,250 hours and 50 employees/75 miles together. Combined obtains the conjunction through the evidence expansion on its first source hit. That is evidence delivery, not proof the ranker found all three provisions independently.
- **F1, propane end use/date:** Source retrieves the propane list item with its date but loses the exemption lead-in. Both other arms return the lead-in too. Statement-only returns substantially more source text: 3,313 unique characters across three hits versus source's 801; combined returns 1,900. No complete document was returned as one hit.

## Regressions and failed mechanisms

- **R4, chlorine scope:** Source and statements rank the actual `Every commercial motor vehicle transporting any quantity of a Division 2.3 chlorine` provision first. Combined pushes that exact required provision to rank **20**. Its first three results are the chlorine placard item, Division 4.3, and Division 1.2/1.3, each augmented with the same entire placard-classification statement. The separately worded any-quantity scope loses to repeated large lists. One alternate chlorine provision might still be useful to a reader, but it does not replace the frozen requirement and the broader any-quantity applicability. This is both redundant-hit cost and a measured relevant-source regression.
- **F3, service equipment:** Source ranks the service prohibition second but omits the certified-equipment child. Statements rank a complete grouped service statement first. Combined ranks the service prohibition fourth: unrelated venting/substitute material occupies the first three places. It therefore loses both selected service and equipment fragments at rank 3.
- **L2, old job after military service:** All arms fail at rank 3. Source's first relevant evidence is rank 10, statements rank 7, combined rank 4. They favor general eligibility/worked-hours material instead of the long-break employment rule. This lexical setup does not connect all everyday terms (`military`, `old job`) to USERRA/service-break language. The improved rank under combined still fails the frozen retrieval limit.
- **L5, falling headcount:** All three return the selected prohibition, but source ranks it second and both other arms third. Refrigerant release is an irrelevant result interleaved with leave hits. Matching ordinary words such as `after` supplies noisy cross-document overlap in the unchanged ranker.

## Labels do not prove complete answers

- **F2 passes the frozen selected-fragment metric in all arms, but the source-only result is insufficient for a full explanation.** It contains `good faith` and `De minimis`, yet the separate compliance routes (i) and (ii) are missing from its first three hits. The label did not require those routes. Do not present 9/14/13 as counts of legally complete answers. This is an under-specified provisional label discovered on raw review; the original label and score remain unchanged. A later evaluation needs a more complete source-support specification before scoring.
- F3's `certified` fragment is also a weak selector by itself. Its full source sentence specifies certification for the refrigerant/appliance type and a reference. The raw statement output has that detail, but the simple metric does not check all of it.
- R1/R2 source results happen to include the `A stop need not be made at` parent in their three slots. The required parent is not guaranteed by the source representation. Combined returns it but expands the result to multiple exemption statements. An exception's stopping effect must not be interpreted as waiving listening, looking or the gear prohibition; retrieval support is not executable applicability logic.
- R3 statement-only ranks the exemption statement ahead of the separate sign-authority statement; its main evidence is broad enough to contain the authority sentence, so the first-support rank is 1. That rank measures *available evidence*, not whether the first displayed summary answers the question.

## Cost, duplication and controls

No full source document enters any individual returned hit. The largest unique source return across all three hits is 2,564 source / 3,313 statement / 2,750 combined characters. Consequently the preregistered 6,000-character cap never binds; equal scores under that cap are not evidence of success under a tight context budget.

Combined indexes 99,415 characters versus source's 15,928. This is largely automatic attachment of the same long grouped statement to many overlapping source items. Returned evidence includes distinct field roles and overlapping spans. Across the 16 queries, it serializes 57,276 repeated source characters beyond the 24,029 unique ones, even after exact evidence identity/field deduplication in existing `records`. This is a real display/prompt payload concern; it is not a measured billed-token cost.

The constructed all-statements-removed control leaves all 85 source and combined rows intact; statement-only has zero. The constructed duplicated-first-statement control creates four extra statement rows, while source/combined row count and indexed texts remain unchanged because combined concatenation exact-deduplicates equal strings. That protects against exact duplicate revisions, but does not stop the broader repeated-context crowding shown in R4. These controls are mechanics, not additional provider failures or a production omission rate.
