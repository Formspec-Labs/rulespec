## Drug manufacturing: 21 CFR 211.188 and 211.192

- D1: Batch production/control records are prepared for each produced batch and
  contain complete production/control information, not merely retained on request.
- D2: The master-record reproduction is accurate, checked, dated and signed.
- D3: Documentation covers accomplishment of each significant manufacturing,
  processing, packing or holding step. The thirteen listed contents are separately
  referenceable with their batch-record framing, rather than a generic list pointer.
- D4: Contents preserve dates; individual major equipment/lines; each component
  or in-process batch identity; weights/measures; in-process and laboratory results;
  packaging/labeling-area inspection before and after use; actual and percentage
  theoretical yield at appropriate phases; complete labeling records with specimens
  or copies; containers/closures; sampling; personnel; investigations; examinations.
- D5: Personnel identification preserves performers and direct supervisors or
  checkers; the alternative for automated equipment under 211.68 identifies the
  human checker. Do not turn the alternative into cumulative mandatory staffing.
- D6: Records include investigations under 211.192 and examinations under 211.134.
  211.192 is present in the grouped source; 211.134 and 211.68 bodies are absent.
  References to absent bodies must not be embellished with invented requirements.
- D7: The quality control unit reviews AND approves all production/control records,
  including packaging/labeling, for compliance with established approved written
  procedures BEFORE release OR distribution.
- D8: Thorough investigation covers unexplained discrepancies (including yield
  outside established minimum/maximum) OR any batch/component specification failure,
  regardless of whether the batch was already distributed.
- D9: That investigation extends to other batches of the same product AND other
  drug products that may be associated with the particular failure/discrepancy.
  Its default statement should identify which investigation is meant.
- D10: A written investigation record includes conclusions AND followup.
- Counterexamples: release timing governs review/approval, not a deadline after
  which investigations stop. Do not invent an explicit quality-control-unit actor
  for every passive duty just because the review sentence names that unit.
  Historical amendment credits are not new manufacturing obligations.

## Aviation: 14 CFR 91.123 and 91.125

- A1: A pilot in command with an obtained clearance must not deviate UNLESS amended
  clearance, emergency, OR a traffic-alert/collision-avoidance resolution advisory.
  Preserve all three alternatives and the clearance-qualified setting.
- A2: IFR flight-plan cancellation is permitted in VFR weather EXCEPT Class A
  airspace; do not make the cancellation mandatory.
- A3: An uncertain pilot must immediately request ATC clarification.
- A4: Operation contrary to an ATC instruction is prohibited where ATC is exercised,
  except in an emergency. Do not automatically import A1's other exceptions here.
- A5: Emergency/resolution-advisory deviations require the pilot in command to
  notify ATC as soon as possible; both triggering alternatives survive.
- A6: Emergency ATC priority, even without deviation from a subpart rule, requires
  a detailed report within 48 hours to the ATC facility manager IF ATC requests it.
  Do not apply this timing/recipient/request condition to A5's notification.
- A7: Using another aircraft pilot's radar-ATC clearance/instruction is prohibited
  unless ATC authorizes it.
- A8: Retain each table mapping with its aircraft setting: steady green = takeoff
  on surface / land in flight; flashing green = taxi / return for landing followed
  by steady green at proper time; steady red = stop / give way and continue circling;
  flashing red = clear runway / airport unsafe, do not land; flashing white = return
  to starting point on airport / not applicable; alternating red/green = extreme
  caution in both settings.
- Counterexamples: "not applicable" is not an airborne command. A surface clearance
  is not an airborne clearance. Historical amendments/OMB approval are not current
  pilot reporting duties. Table entries may use one complete dual-setting record
  per signal or separate setting records; raw count alone does not choose a winner.

## Information safeguards: 45 CFR 164.310 and 164.312

External 164.306 and 164.308(a)(4) are not supplied. Do not infer that Addressable
means optional, or treat it as identical to Required. Preserve the source's
qualification explicitly; judging the missing external policy is out of scope.

- H1: Covered entity OR business associate remains the actor, qualified by 164.306.
- H2: Facility access is limited while properly authorized access remains allowed.
- H3: Four addressable facility specifications survive: emergency restoration access
  under both named plans (implement as needed); safeguarding facility/equipment
  against unauthorized physical access/tampering/theft; role/function-based access
  validation including visitors and software testing/revision; records of security-
  related physical repairs/modifications.
- H4: Workstation-use policies specify proper functions, manner and physical
  surroundings for a workstation/class accessing electronic protected health
  information. Workstation physical security separately restricts authorized users.
- H5: Device/media policies govern receipt/removal into/out of facilities AND
  movement within facilities, for hardware/media containing protected information.
- H6: Required disposal handles final disposition of the information and/or its
  hardware/media; required reuse removes that information BEFORE media reuse.
- H7: Addressable accountability records movements and responsible people;
  addressable backup makes a retrievable EXACT copy WHEN NEEDED before movement.
- H8: Technical access policies cover systems maintaining electronic protected
  health information and only people/software with granted 164.308(a)(4) rights.
- H9: Required unique identity uses a unique name and/or number for identification
  and tracking; required emergency access obtains needed information during an
  emergency, implementing procedures as needed.
- H10: Addressable automatic logoff occurs after predetermined inactivity;
  addressable encryption/decryption preserves BOTH operations.
- H11: Audit controls record AND examine activity, using hardware, software and/or
  procedural mechanisms in systems containing OR using protected information.
- H12: Integrity prevents improper alteration/destruction; its addressable
  authentication mechanism corroborates no unauthorized alteration/destruction.
- H13: Person/entity authentication verifies the access seeker's claimed identity.
- H14: Transmission security covers information sent over electronic networks.
  Addressable integrity detects improper modification until disposal; addressable
  transmission encryption retains "whenever deemed appropriate."
- Counterexamples: physical facility access and technical information-system access
  are distinct. Hardware movement timing is not a logoff/encryption trigger.
  Addressable qualifications must not be silently dropped from standalone duties;
  the missing external policy limits what this experiment can establish.
