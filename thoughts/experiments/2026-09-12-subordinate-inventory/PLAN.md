# Does explicit passage accounting improve missing-detail detection?

Decision: Determine whether requiring an inventory decision for every supplied
source passage is worth broader evaluation before changing production. The current
audit missed three CSBG detail-gap groups because its independent inventory also
compressed those meanings. No production adoption or automatic repairs in this test.

Hypotheses and distinguishing predictions:
- H1: Free inventory allocation compresses subordinate meaning. Explicit passage
  accounting retains more of that meaning, and unchanged comparison detects the
  omissions in the same draft.
- H2: Passage accounting increases unit counts but still compresses meaning within
  passages. Structural adherence rises without semantic improvement.
- H3: More complete inventory is produced, but comparison still credits incomplete
  draft statements or treats necessary conditions independently. Inventory improves;
  diagnostic usefulness does not. Prior expanded-context tests showed this risk.
- H4: Explicit passage accounting invents independent duties or duplicates parent
  meanings, leading to false alarms on definitions, optional examples and conditions.

Arms: A is the unchanged production source-first inventory. B uses the SAME full
source, passage catalog, inventory schema and settings, adding a source-ordered
checklist that requires a separate inventory decision for every nonempty F passage.
Headings and bare markers may be background; substantive components retain their
governing lead-ins in meaning/scope_refs. No new Core/profile schema or parser.
Both inventories feed the unchanged comparison against the same frozen draft.
This tests the instruction/checklist bundle, not hidden model attention. An ignored
checklist remains a failed intervention observation; no repair/retry is allowed.

Cases:
1. Saved focused CSBG section 9908 draft/window, unchanged from the focus experiment.
   Targets: (b)(1) immediate/urgent emergency needs and urban-intervention/widespread-
   replication details; (b)(3) information/referrals/case management/followup methods;
   (b)(5) explicit low-income service recipients. Controls: (b)(6), (9), (10), (11),
   (12) retain where-appropriate, maximum-extent-possible, petition conditions,
   Secretary-request and performance alternatives. Do not demand duplicate parent
   rules when child records jointly preserve their meaning.
2. New native USLM excerpt 20 USC 1414(d)(1), complete definitions/team/attendance
   provisions. Check conditional preschool and alternate-assessment content,
   both alternate-assessment reasons, service date/frequency/location/duration,
   transition timing and annual update, written consent/input prerequisites,
   parent-request transition invitation. Controls: peer-reviewed research only to
   extent practicable; optional team members; qualified regular-teacher requirement;
   no obligation to add extra or duplicate IEP information; periodic-report examples
   must not become mandatory quarterly reporting. Preserve definition versus duty.
3. New native USLM excerpt 20 USC 6312(b), complete plan provisions. Check all four
   monitoring components, teacher-disparity groups/types, homeless enrollment/
   attendance/success, consultation participants, transition alternatives and
   career/work-based-learning details. Controls: where appropriate in (5), if
   applicable in (8)/(10), optional examples in (11), agency discretion in (12)/(13).
   Remote references identify unavailable scope; do not fabricate their content.

Freshness: targeted preflight search found no 1414/6312 cases in experiment/example
PLAN/RESULTS notes. These are selected new excerpts in one domain, not an independent
benchmark, and cannot establish accuracy across government documents. Use the same
local official USLM archive release 119-102 as CSBG; preserve member and serialized
subtree hashes and exact prepared text. The excerpts are not complete sections or
claims about current law. New model outputs are preserved without constructed errors.

Before audit calls, make one normal low-thinking extraction of each fresh excerpt,
manually review the raw output against the pinned source, then save claim-specific
prelabels. Source-based criteria above stay fixed. If a fresh draft has no relevant
defects, retain it as a false-positive control and mark sensitivity there unresolved.
Do not replace cases or seed artificial defects after viewing outputs.

Held constant: gemini-3.8-flash, temperature 0, one candidate, no numeric thinking
budget. Fresh extraction uses current defaults (low, 16,384 output tokens, 24,000
focus chars); audits use medium thinking and 32,768 output tokens. Full fixed focus
and context identical between each pair. One observation per arm per case. Randomize
inventory/comparison order and anonymous review labels. Inventories may reveal the
arm; masking is partial and manual judgments remain revisable.

Bound: at most 14 provider calls (2 extraction, 6 inventory, 6 comparison), 20 minutes
of provider work or 350,000 recorded tokens. Stop starting calls at the first bound;
an in-flight call may finish over it. No retries, prompt tuning, replacement cases
or repairs. Failed extraction removes its downstream case; failed/unusable inventory
skips its comparison. Keep failures and partial responses in accounting.

Record exact inputs, runtime/config, actual SDK requests, raw responses, schema/
grounding refusals, source maps and model versions. Reuse existing capture, parser,
compiler and audit functions. This is a selected-window experiment, not ordinary
whole-document audit accounting. Freeze upstream inputs before dependent calls.

Measures: checklist adherence; retained subordinate meaning with correct scope/
force; specific supported gap findings (an unrelated error is not a hit); misses;
false alarms/false obligations on faithful material; reciprocal links and grounded
parse validity; provider input/output/thinking tokens and elapsed time separately.
More units, quotations or successfully processed passages do not prove completeness.

Decision rule: recommend broader evaluation only if B retains and enables detection
of all three known CSBG gap groups, improves a prelabelled detail omission on at
least one fresh excerpt relative to contemporaneous A, and introduces no confirmed
substantive false alarm or false obligation on the named controls. If inventory
improves but comparison does not, report bounded inventory benefit and not solved.
If the fresh drafts lack defects, downstream improvement is unresolved even if
controls pass. No failed wider gate can be relabeled as production approval.
Stop and consolidate at the bound, including costs and remaining uncertainty.
